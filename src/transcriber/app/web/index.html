Set the language only if you are sure. Given the wrong one the model will not warn you — it will improvise: the text stays fluent, the facts do not.

Say how many voices you expect, if you know. Counting them is the weakest half of the labelling, and it errs by splitting one voice into several: a recording of three people came back as nine. A number you know beats any guess; leave it empty and the count is guessed.

Speaker labels work out of the box, on openly published models. They name every voice they hear, but a brief question from the room tends to be merged into the speaker who was talking.

pyannote keeps those apart. Its models are free, but the terms have to be accepted by hand and a token created — nothing an installer can do for you:

  1. accept the terms at the model page
  2. create a read token at huggingface.co/settings/tokens
  3. paste it here
